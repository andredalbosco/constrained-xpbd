# Objective revisions for independent position and velocity

Two revised position objectives restore the expected local mechanics in the tested scalar and moving multidimensional states. At 120 Hz over 10 seconds, their position RMS errors are about 0.0022 m for the orbiting spring, 0.0016 m for the elastic pendulum, and 0.004 m for the two-spring chain. The original midpoint-target objective can conserve energy and converge tightly while following substantially wrong motion.

The new mechanism is identifiable: at a generic moving state, the original midpoint-target objective doubles the component of acceleration perpendicular to velocity. Decreasing velocity compliance does not remove that component. The corrected objectives change the mechanical target itself.

## Scope and reproducible artifacts

This directory contains 22 explicitly defined objective compositions in `objectives.hpp`:

- Original endpoint/midpoint objectives with constant beta controls.
- Original midpoint objectives with beta = c(h omega)² or c(h omega)⁴, and constant 0.1/0.01 comparisons.
- A derived quarter-acceleration predictor with quarter-weight endpoint potential.
- A midpoint-potential objective.
- Fixed-target projections from XPBD, second-order Verlet/RATTLE, or midpoint discrete-gradient mechanics, with energy and optional angular momentum.
- Squared mechanical residual objectives using midpoint or discrete-gradient forces, with optional hard energy/angular momentum.

In these new variant IDs, `_E` denotes hard energy and `_EL` denotes hard energy plus angular momentum L. The original EL/LE solver schedules keep their separate round-one meaning.

The frequency scale is a model-dependent upper estimate: `sqrt(4 k_max / m_min)` for compliant springs and `sqrt(max(|g|/L_min,1))` for rods. It is independent of instantaneous velocity and oscillation amplitude. Beta is floored at 1e-12 for numerical evaluation. This floor is recorded through the actual beta columns; it is not presented as an exact asymptotic method.

## Why shrinking beta misses a multidimensional defect

Consider the original objective with independent endpoint velocity,

\[
F(x,v)=\frac{1}{2h^2}\|x-x_n-hv_n\|_M^2+U(x)
+\frac{1}{2\beta}\left\|v+v_n-2\frac{x-x_n}{h}\right\|_M^2,
\]

subject to exact total energy. Write

\[
a_n=-M^{-1}\nabla U(x_n),\qquad
x=x_n+hv_n+h^2 A+O(h^3),\qquad
v=v_n+hB+O(h^2).
\]

For a fixed nonzero initial velocity, the leading stationary conditions, with energy multiplier \(\mu=h\mu_1+O(h^2)\), are

\[
A-a_n-\frac{2}{\beta}(B-2A)=0,
\qquad
\frac{B-2A}{\beta}+\mu_1v_n=0,
\qquad
v_n^TM(B-a_n)=0.
\]

The last equation is the leading exact-energy balance. Solving gives

\[
\boxed{
B=2a_n-
\frac{v_n^TMa_n}{v_n^TMv_n}\,v_n.
}
\]

The acceleration parallel to velocity is correct at leading order; its mass-orthogonal component is doubled. Beta cancels. In particular, the initial orbiting spring has tangential velocity and radial force, so this objective predicts twice the physical radial acceleration even when beta tends to zero with the timestep.

The angle probes test this beyond that one special case. With physical acceleration magnitude 10 m/s², speed 0.8 m/s and h=1/5120 s, measured acceleration errors for angles 0°, 30°, 60°, and 90° are 0.0054, 4.9709, 8.6310, and 9.9999 m/s². The predicted limiting errors are 0, 5, 8.6603, and 10 m/s². Errors relative to the biased prediction decrease approximately fourfold when h decreases fourfold.

This expansion assumes nonzero velocity before taking h to zero. The probes also include zero speed and 1e-4 m/s, and report `h_acceleration_over_speed`. If h|a| is comparable to or greater than |v|, the generic-state expansion is not a uniform approximation. The scalar turning-point calculation is a separate limit.

## Deriving a corrected position objective

Allow a predictor coefficient p and potential coefficient w:

\[
\Phi_{p,w}(x)=
\frac{1}{2h^2}
\|x-x_n-hv_n-p h^2a_n\|_M^2+wU(x).
\]

With midpoint velocity consistency, the unconstrained position stationarity equation corresponds to

\[
v-v_n=2h\,[p a_n+w a(x)].
\]

Matching the leading physical acceleration requires p+w=1/2. Since

\[
a(x)=a_n-hM^{-1}\nabla^2U(x_n)v_n+O(h^2),
\]

matching the second-order velocity term requires w=1/4 and p=1/4. This yields

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_Q(x)=
\frac{1}{2h^2}
\|x-x_n-hv_n-\tfrac14h^2a_n\|_M^2
+\tfrac14U(x).
}
\]

It produces trapezoidal force mechanics when the midpoint velocity relation holds exactly. A half-acceleration predictor plus half-weight endpoint potential has p+w=1 and is included as an intentionally inconsistent control, not treated as a derived physical method.

A second consistent choice is

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_M(x)=
\frac{1}{2h^2}\|x-x_n-hv_n\|_M^2
+U\!\left(\frac{x+x_n}{2}\right).
}
\]

Its position derivative contains half the midpoint potential gradient, giving implicit-midpoint force mechanics with the midpoint velocity relation. Both revised objectives retain the same independent-velocity penalty and hard endpoint-energy constraint as the controls.

For a quadratic scalar spring, their mechanical solution is also an unconstrained stationary minimum of the objective, has zero velocity penalty, and already conserves energy. Thus the hard-energy row does not need to redirect the motion. The two objectives differ only by an irrelevant constant in this scalar quadratic case. This holds for moving initial states as well as release from rest.

Measured 160-to-320-Hz local velocity-error orders for `quarter_b1` are 2.997 at scalar release, 3.011 for the moving scalar state, and 2.998 for the orbiting spring. The original midpoint objective remains first order in one-step velocity error for the orbiting state, even with the timestep-dependent beta schedules. These are local consistency measurements, not a general proof of stability or a guarantee for arbitrary systems.

Dense scalar searches cover the entire constant-energy ellipse at 65,536 angles and refine its best sampled interval. Four KKT seeds are the mechanical target, original implicit target, old state, and velocity-reflected target. For quarter/midpoint-potential beta=1, the numerical objective gaps to the independently searched minima stay at floating-point scale across the tested release and moving states. Shrinking-beta controls can also reach their searched minima; that does not repair their multidimensional mechanical target.

## Mechanical residual and fixed-target alternatives

The residual objectives use

\[
r_k=\frac{x-x_n}{h}-\frac{v+v_n}{2},
\qquad
r_p=v-v_n-hM^{-1}\bar f,
\qquad
F_{LS}=\tfrac12\|r_k\|_M^2+\tfrac12\|r_p\|_M^2.
\]

For springs, the force is either the ordinary midpoint force or the potential-exact discrete-gradient force. The latter lies along the midpoint edge and gives exact elastic work. If both residuals vanish, momentum and kinematics close mechanically; energy is then already consistent for the discrete-gradient construction. Hard energy is tested as an additional constraint, rather than asserted to follow from a force estimate without momentum closure. Rigid versions carry independent reaction coefficients and enforce endpoint position and tangency.

Fixed-target projection minimizes a mass-weighted displacement from a frozen target state, with position changes measured in velocity units by division by h. Targets are XPBD, velocity Verlet (RATTLE for rods), or midpoint discrete-gradient mechanics. A midpoint-mechanics target already performs the physical solve, so its projection should not be presented as a cheaper new integrator. Optional angular momentum is imposed only for the 2D central spring without gravity; gravity cases instead report torque-balance error.

## Matched 120-Hz, 10-second results

All rows below use certified references and complete trajectories. Values are position RMS in metres.

| Environment / initial condition | Original midpoint, beta=(h omega)² | Quarter objective, beta=1 | Midpoint potential, beta=1 | DG residual + hard energy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Scalar release | 0.16959 | 0.005941 | 0.005941 | 0.005941 |
| 2D spring orbit | 1.20117 | 0.002214 | 0.002176 | 0.002223 |
| Rigid pendulum release | 0.03683 | 0.000518 | 0.000827 | 0.000518 |
| Elastic pendulum mixed motion | 0.56461 | 0.001596 | 0.001562 | 0.001599 |
| Two-spring chain mixed motion | 0.55149 | 0.004237 | 0.003850 | 0.004205 |

The original beta(h) column preserves energy to roughly a few parts in 1e9 in these cases. Its large trajectory error therefore survives tight energy and KKT residuals. The revised objectives also preserve energy closely in this experiment, with meaningful motion improvement.

Angular momentum separates otherwise similar energy-preserving methods. On the 10-second spring orbit, the maximum angular error is approximately 0.411 for the original beta(h) objective, 1.15e-4 for the quarter objective, 8.36e-5 for midpoint potential, and 2.44e-9 for the DG mechanical residual. Adding angular momentum to the Verlet-target projection reduces its angular error from 9.54e-4 to 7.3e-14, but changes its trajectory error; it does not uniformly improve every metric at every horizon.

At 60 seconds, quarter/midpoint-potential errors remain much smaller than the original controls for the certified scalar, orbit, rigid and elastic cases. The mixed two-spring chain has materially larger long-horizon trajectory differences (roughly 0.24–0.28 m for these revised methods), despite small energy errors. The large-chain 60-second reference fails the paired-resolution test by up to 0.150 m, so its trajectory RMS is not used to rank methods.

## Rigid endpoint tangency requires a finite kinematic defect

The revised rod solves enforce both endpoint length and endpoint velocity tangency. They allow the midpoint position relation to have its natural finite-step error. For a kicked rod at h=1/1280 s, the quarter objective and DG residual formulation have length/tangency errors near 1e-16, a nonzero kinetic-energy change about −0.00556, and midpoint position defects about 8.48e-10 m. The physical high-resolution trajectory has a defect about 9.55e-10 m.

This avoids imposing exact midpoint position kinematics together with endpoint tangency, a combination that would force equal endpoint speeds on a fixed circle. It also distinguishes these constrained-state formulations from a Cartesian midpoint rod scheme that carries an off-tangent velocity mode.

## Near-rest and numerical limits

Near-rest tests use scalar extensions down to 2.5e-7 m and include exact equilibrium. All exact-equilibrium cases remain stationary. The frequency-based beta schedule stays defined when velocity vanishes, but a very stiff penalty still damages numerical conditioning: the original h⁴ schedule reaches relative energy error 0.106 in the near-rest grid and frequently misses residual thresholds. Corrected fixed-beta objectives show much smaller errors; their worst relative energy errors in this grid are around 4e-6. Tiny-amplitude normalization and cancellation also limit strict residual passage for some revised runs, so these are not claimed uniformly roundoff-exact.

No speed comparisons are claimed: the study ran concurrently with other round-two experiments. `compute_ms` is diagnostic only.

## Reproduction

The headers use include paths rather than absolute repository paths. With the round-one suite available as `composition_2026_10_31` and Eigen on the include path, compile these targets:

```text
objective_study       <- run.cpp             (C++17, OpenMP)
objective_near_rest   <- near_rest.cpp        (C++17)
objective_moving_probe<- moving_probe.cpp     (C++17)
```

Run with this directory as the working directory; CSVs and traces are written there:

```bash
objective_study --validate-only
objective_study --local-only
objective_study --trajectories-only
objective_near_rest
objective_moving_probe
python3 analyze.py
```

`objective_study` without an option runs validation, local/dense studies, and principal trajectories. Analysis requires NumPy and Matplotlib. The experiment leaves the round-one implementation unchanged.
